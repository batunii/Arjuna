1
The idea · starting from something you already do

We already delete distraction for space and for sound. Almost never for sight.

Space

The library carrel

Wooden walls on three sides. Nothing is added to help you read — the room is simply subtracted until only the page is left.

Sound

Noise-cancelling headphones

ANC does not make the lecture louder. It makes everything else quieter. A mass-market product whose entire function is removal.

Sight

Arjuna and the sparrow's eye

In the Mahābhārata, Droṇa asks his students what they see before they shoot. Only Arjuna answers: "only the eye of the bird." The oldest description of perfect visual focus — everything else gone.

Three thousand years of aspiring to it, and for vision we still build the opposite: arrows, highlights, labels, pop-ups. Every visual attention aid on the market adds something to the scene. This project builds the third column instead.

The acoustic analogy is not just rhetoric — it is the literature's own frame. Visual Noise Cancellation proposes a head-worn display that "actively moderates the visual environment," attenuating noise and passing signal, and notes that the ANC architecture (microphone → processing → speaker) maps exactly onto a passthrough headset's camera → shader → display. This dissertation can be read as the first software-defined VNC system on consumer hardware.
Sources · Visual Noise Cancellation (ACM, c. 2024). Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva — Droṇa's archery test.
2
Vocabulary · four terms, then everything else follows

Augmented Reality adds. Diminished Reality takes away.

Diminished Reality (DR)
The family of techniques that conceal, eliminate, or see through real objects in a mediated view. Established as a field by Mori, Ikeda & Saito's 2017 survey. Most classical DR work removes whole objects by inpainting; this project instead attenuates regions, trading semantic precision for real-time feasibility.
Video see-through (VST) passthrough
On Quest 3 you never look at the room directly. Outward cameras capture it, the headset reconstructs it, and you see a screen. Because every pixel is synthesised, it can in principle be changed — which is why subtraction is possible here and not on transparent glasses.
Eccentricity
Angular distance from where you are looking, in degrees of visual angle. Your thumbnail at arm's length is about 1.5°. Everything in this talk is measured in these units, because the visual system's properties change with them.
The periphery
Not "blurry central vision" but a differently tuned system: highly sensitive to motion and flicker, poor at detail and colour. That asymmetry is the whole opportunity — degrade the right channel and it is barely noticed.
Focus window
The region this system spares. The user paints it with the controller; it locks to the room, not to the head. Everything outside it is the periphery the filter acts on.
Attention tunnelling
The named failure mode of aggressive guidance: attention locks so hard onto the cued region that genuinely important events elsewhere are missed. Identified by Biocca et al. This is the risk the whole evaluation is built to detect.
Sources · Mori, Ikeda & Saito (2017), A survey of diminished reality, IPSJ TCVA 9(17). Biocca et al., attention issues in spatial information systems. Stewart et al. (2020) on peripheral–foveal interaction.
3
Before building anything · what would have to be true

"Dimming the clutter should help" is an intuition. Three things have to be true for it to be a project.

Claim 1

The clutter actually costs something

If peripheral distractors do not measurably damage performance, there is nothing to recover and the idea is cosmetic. This has to be an established number, not an assumption.

Claim 2

Removing it returns capacity

And specifically: which removal? Dark? Grey? Blurred? Outlined? These are different manipulations of different visual channels and there is no reason to expect them to work equally.

Claim 3

It can be built at all

On hardware people already own — not a bench rig with a beam-splitter. If subtraction needs custom optics, it is a lab curiosity rather than an interface.

So the first work of the dissertation was not code. It was going through the literature — roughly 110 sources — to find out whether anyone had already answered these, and what they found when they tried.

Two of the three came back supported. The second came back with something more useful than support: a set of results showing that specific ways of subtracting reality actively fail. Those negative findings did more to shape this system than the positive ones did — they are the next two slides, and they are why the mode family looks the way it does.
Corpus: ~110 sources across ISMAR, CHI, UIST, IEEE VR, DIS, SAP, Human Factors, Frontiers and arXiv, 1998–2026. Thematic core in lit-review-48papers.md; full corpus map in the project record.
4
Evidence for · claim 1 and claim 2, in the literature's own numbers

The cost is real, and it is measured in errors — not in speed.

The target to beat

Peripheral distractors in a virtual classroom, N = 66

On a Go/No-go sustained-attention task, adding peripheral visual distractors raised commission errors 1.33 → 3.15 (p < .001) and omission errors 0.14 → 1.18 (p < .001) — while leaving mean reaction time unchanged.

This one result does double duty: it establishes that the cost exists, and it tells us errors, not latency, are the sensitive currency. That fixed the primary measure of our own study before we collected anything.

The closest published analogue

DR attenuation of distractors, two within-subject experiments

McLaughlin et al. (Human Factors, 2025): participants performed a demanding assembly task under distraction with DR-style attenuation. Universal attenuation — structurally the closest condition to a peripheral vignette — produced fewer errors (β = −0.37, p = .021) and lower subjective workload than clever context-aware removal. Simpler beat smarter.

Mechanism

Cognitive Load Theory

Irrelevant visual information consumes the same limited working memory the task needs (extraneous load). Diminishment is an extraneous-load intervention: it does not make you smarter, it removes competing demands at the perceptual source.

Mechanism

Salience maps · Itti, Koch & Niebur (1998)

Attention goes where local centre–surround contrast is highest. Contrast is the master variable: raise it and you attract gaze, flatten it and you repel it. A periphery whose contrast is flattened stops issuing capture events at all.

Dose–response

Duan et al. (2022) · SARD dataset

AR overlay opacity predictably modulates where attention lands — so an alpha-blended overlay is an attention instrument with a measurable dose curve, not a visual style.

Convergent

DiminishAR (2025) · FocalSpace (2013)

Camouflaging one distracting phone restored working-memory performance to a level statistically comparable with physically removing it (N = 60). Thirteen years earlier, synthetic background blur in video calls improved memory for meeting content.

Sources · Impact of Visual Distractors in VR on Sustained Attention, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience (2025), N = 66 within-subject. McLaughlin et al. (2025), Human Factors, doi:10.1177/00187208251325169. Sweller-tradition CLT via Buchner et al. Itti, Koch & Niebur (1998), IEEE TPAMI 20(11). Duan et al. (2022). Lee & Kim, DiminishAR (2025). Yao, DeVincenzi, Pereira & Ishii, FocalSpace (2013).
5
Evidence against · the findings that pruned the design

Five results say the obvious ways of doing this do not work.

Kills whole-periphery blur

Grogorick et al. (2018) · N = 102, immersive dome

A five-way comparison of guidance techniques. SpatialBlur — blurring the whole periphery, the most intuitive "soften the distraction" design — was the least subtle of all five and actively irritated 59 of 102 participants. Their conclusion is blunt: "no method remains completely unnoticed."

Kills desaturation as a mode

Eye-tracked comparison in 360° video (IEEE, 2021)

Head-to-head of area darkening, context-based darkening and desaturation for steering attention. Area darkening guided attention effectively; desaturation alone had almost no guidance effect. Greying out the surroundings — the other intuitive design — barely moves attention at all.

Kills head-coupled strength

Norouzi, Bruder & Welch (2018)

A vignette whose strength is coupled to head rotation — dim more as you turn, the natural way to build it — increased simulator sickness relative to no vignette at all. A directly actionable negative: the obvious coupling is the wrong sign.

Kills blackout-as-default

Cheng et al. (CHI 2022) · Cao et al. (2021) · Barhorst-Cates et al. (2016)

Asked how they want reality diminished, users preferred partial opacity over complete removal and wanted context-preserving residue — outlines showing something is there. Sparse noise "grains" beat opaque black on search performance, with 15 of 20 preferring grains. And anxiety is elevated in all field-restricted conditions, rising monotonically as the field narrows.

And an honest negative from the work this project descends from: Sutton et al. (UIST 2022) found their saliency modulation of the real world drew significantly more fixations to targets (χ² = 25.57, p < .001) but was not imperceptible — participants noticed. The subtlety dream is unavailable. This project therefore stopped chasing invisibility and designed for noticeable but tolerable instead.
Sources · Grogorick, Albuquerque & Magnor (2018) + companion in ACM TAP. Area-darkening vs desaturation comparison, IEEE (2021). Norouzi, Bruder & Welch (2018), ACM SAP. Cheng, Yin, Yan, Gugenheimer & Lindlbauer, CHI 2022, doi:10.1145/3491102.3517452. Cao, Grandi & Kopper (2021). Barhorst-Cates, Rand & Creem-Regehr (2016). The research gap · what nobody had done

Five gaps fall out of the review. This dissertation takes four of them.

#Gap in the literatureStatus here
1No unified multi-mode DR system. Diminishment effects have been studied one at a time; no prior work puts several DR guidance modes in one configurable system so they can be compared within a single design.addressed Eleven modes in one shader, spanning the design space; three carried into evaluation.
2No hybrid of system passthrough and app-processed camera feed in one view. Global colour restyling of system passthrough exists; full pixel control of an inferior camera feed exists; nobody composites the two — and nobody implements peripheral passthrough manipulation for attention on a standalone consumer headset.addressed The window-as-absence hybrid — the strongest and most precisely scoped novelty claim.
3No temporal transition mechanics. DR is always evaluated as a static state. Gradual formation, easing, and motion-contingent suppression have never been formally parameterised — despite evidence that slow ramps evade awareness and that head-coupled strengthening harms comfort.partial Formation ramps and inverted motion-coupling implemented, parameterised and grounded; isolated evaluation is future work.
4No user-controlled DR intensity. User-initiated graded release and re-application of diminishment is unstudied.groundwork Users get spatial control (painted window) but not intensity control. Future work.
5No dual-channel DR technique. Desaturation and blur have been studied separately, never combined and parameterised as one compound channel.not claimed The compound channel exists inside ColorPop, but the study deliberately tests the unconfounded dark modes.
Gap 2 is the technical heart of the dissertation, and gaps 1 and 3 are consequences of building the system that closes it. Gap 5 is the one I explicitly declined: a compound effect whose ingredients cannot be separated is a bad thing to hang a confirmatory claim on.

Position · the two closest published studies, and the delta

Both nearest neighbours simulated diminished reality inside VR — because they had to.

Neighbour 1

Cheng et al. · CHI 2022

Established what users want from diminishment (N = 16, N = 12). Ran entirely inside VR simulations of DR, because no deployable DR hardware existed. A preference study of an imagined system.

Neighbour 2

McLaughlin et al. · Human Factors 2025

Established that distractor attenuation improves task performance. Also inside a virtual environment, with the diminishment simulated by the VR scene itself.

The delta

This work

The piece both were missing: a working diminishment system on real consumer passthrough hardware. Plus a design that deliberately pairs a real-hardware block against a simulation block within the same participants.

Why that pairing is worth doing. If the real-hardware block and the simulation block agree in direction, the field learns that the simulation methodology Cheng and McLaughlin were forced into predicts real-hardware outcomes — which retro-validates a decade of work. If they diverge, the divergence itself is a finding about what simulation misses. Either answer is publishable.
And the appetite is documented. A 2026 co-design study with fifteen students with ADHD developed exactly this concept — blur, desaturation and removal as user-configurable filters — through diary studies and workshops, and built no prototype. A CHI 2026 Vision Pro study obscured specific people to ease social discomfort, but as targeted occlusion, not peripheral guidance. Demand is established; implementation is not.
Sources · Cheng et al., CHI 2022. McLaughlin et al., Human Factors (2025). Exploring Diminished Reality for Attention Support, DIS 2026. Obscuring Undesirable Individuals…, CHI 2026. Also adjacent: Yantaç et al. on DR walls for autistic users; Murphy et al. (2021, 2022) on DR as a training scaffold.

Novelty · scoped narrowly enough to defend

Every ingredient has precedent. The composite has none.

Precedent, cited not claimed

Camera feed on geometry with GPU effects

QuestCameraKit — but always as content within the scene, never a full-surround treated periphery.

A head-centred sphere revealing passthrough

Meta's own "Passthrough Transitioning" motif — but the sphere carries virtual content for scene fades, not a processed camera feed.

Alpha punch-through windows

Documented platform practice; shipped commercially in Immersed's Passthrough Portals (2022) so desk workers could see their keyboards. The hole always reveals passthrough surrounded by virtual content.

Peripheral dimming vignette

Quest OS v67 "Theatre View" — but explicitly not over passthrough. Vision Pro's surroundings dimming is global, not windowed.

Saliency modulation of reality

Sutton et al. (2022) — on a bench-mounted optical see-through rig whose additive optics can only add light.

The claim

What is claimed as novel

Native-quality system passthrough revealed through a world-locked focus window in an effect-processed camera-feed periphery — exploiting the quality and field-of-view asymmetry between the two layers — applied to peripheral attention guidance on a standalone consumer headset. Together with the world-direction sampling that makes a mono feed binocularly viewable.

The disclaimer

What is not claimed

An alpha-blended dark overlay, by itself, is just compositing. It is not novel and this dissertation does not pretend it is. Nor is "DR on passthrough" in general — only the specific composite above.

The technical problem · why this is hard rather than fiddly

You cannot dim the real world, because you are not allowed to read it.

Display — what the user actually seescomposited by the OS
Your application's rendered layerfull control · drawn in front
▲   the compositor combines these   ▲
OS passthrough layer — the reconstructed real worldcannot read · cannot modify · cannot subtract

The reconstructed view of the room is produced and owned by the operating system. It is composited behind application content at a stage of the pipeline applications never reach. It is never handed to your shader as a texture.

So the naive formulation of subtractive DR — sample the passthrough pixel, darken it, write it back — is not difficult on this platform. It is architecturally impossible.

And it is not a bug

The boundary exists for good reasons

Passthrough is a continuous camera view of your home, your workplace, and bystanders. Read access is a privacy exposure; write access is a safety exposure — an app could imperceptibly alter the world you are walking through.

Both concerns strengthen over time. Research that assumes future read–modify–write access is betting against the platform vendors' incentives.

The bet this project makes

Treat the wall as permanent

And ask what can be built inside it. That is what gives the resulting design space a claim to durability rather than a shelf life.

Not just Meta

The other platforms are stricter

On Vision Pro, camera pixels need an enterprise entitlement incompatible with App Store distribution, and the only world-appearance control is global dimming. On true optical see-through glasses the wall is physical: additive optics can add light but cannot remove photons. "Draw black" means "draw nothing."

Sources · Meta Passthrough documentation; surface-projected passthrough deprecated at SDK v83. Apple visionOS enterprise camera-access documentation (2024). Itoh et al. (2021) on occlusion-capable OST optics.

The Tier-1 finding

It is not that nothing can be done — it is that nothing spatial can be done

You can desaturate the entire visual field without touching a camera pixel. But every styling operation applies to the whole layer uniformly. Since attention guidance is definitionally spatial — treating the focus region differently from the periphery is the entire point — Tier 1 alone cannot implement any mode in this dissertation. The absence of spatial control, not the absence of control, is the finding.

Why a taxonomy and not just a system

It makes future platforms locatable

Three walls define the space: no reads at any tier, no spatial modulation of the native layer, no subtraction on optical see-through. Any future platform can be placed in the space by asking which of the three walls it removes. The engineering is platform-bound; the taxonomy is not.

The core technical obstacle · Tier 3's hidden catch

Meta only exposes a sub-part of what you are looking at.

Tier 3 gives you real pixels — but of a different, lower-grade copy of reality. Not the image the user is seeing.

The trap

The naive Tier-3 design makes everything worse

Re-render the whole view from the camera feed and you have accepted, everywhere: mono instead of stereo, 1280×960 instead of native, added latency, and a field of view 23° narrower on each side than the one you replaced.

And the part the camera cannot see is exactly the far periphery — the region a peripheral-attention system most wants to act on.

Plus a measurement ceiling

Passthrough is already below human acuity

Psychophysical measurement shows no current video see-through headset reaches normal human acuity at any light level, with Quest 3 significantly worse in low light. Any task requiring fine real-world detail confounds your manipulation with the medium.

So: how do you apply a per-pixel effect to the periphery without paying camera-grade quality where the user is actually working?

The novel move · one line of shader arithmetic

The highest-fidelity way to render reality is to not render it.

Inside the window: alpha = 0

Nothing is drawn at all. The user receives the operating system's own passthrough at full native quality, the full ~110° field of view, and the OS pipeline's own latency compensation — none of which any application reconstruction can match. Exactly where they are being asked to look.

Outside: the effect budget is spent where acuity is lowest

All manipulation is confined to the periphery. One line of arithmetic simultaneously solves the field-of-view problem (the camera covers only ~87°) and the fidelity problem (mono 1280×960 vs native stereo) — by ensuring the reconstruction is only ever shown where neither limitation matters much.

This inverts the naive design. And it is the general form of a Tier-2 insight worth stating on its own: at Tier 2, the highest-fidelity rendering of reality is an absence of rendering.

The curve is the shipped geometry: a 6°×4° clear core with a 24° soft edge, giving t = smoothstep(0, 24, max(|az|−6, |el|−4, 0)). Slide 21 shows why those numbers, and not others.

Alpha punch-through is documented platform practice and has shipped commercially — but in every prior use the hole reveals passthrough surrounded by virtual content. Using the hole as the focus region of a diminished periphery is, per the prior-art search, unprecedented.

The flagship configuration · composing tiers 2 and 3

Two different renderings of the same reality, fused into one continuous view.

Full-fidelity reality inside the window, surrounded by a re-graded interpretation of reality outside it — exploiting the quality/FOV asymmetry between the two layers rather than suffering it.

The honest seam

The two renderings do not share a latency budget

The window is served by the platform's dedicated low-latency reprojection; the camera-fed periphery carries the application pipeline's extra delay. Static or slow views: invisible. Fast head rotation: the periphery can momentarily lag the window at the seam.

The motion suppression of slide 22 incidentally masks the worst case — the effect fades out precisely when the head moves fast — but the residual mismatch is a real property of the hybrid, not something argued away.

Implementation · one sphere, one shader, one manager

The whole effect is one number, computed per pixel.

An inverted sphere around the head

~10 m radius, rendered inside-out (Cull Front), transparent queue, depth writes off, re-centred on the head every frame — translation only, never rotation, which is what keeps its surface coordinates world-stable. Every fragment the user can see, in any direction, is a fragment of this sphere.

Each fragment works out its own direction

dir = normalize(worldPos − _SphereCenter)
az  = atan2(dir.x, dir.z)
el  = asin(dir.y)

Computed per-fragment from world position rather than interpolated from vertices — which avoids interpolation error at the sphere's poles.

…then its distance outside the window

t = smoothstep(0, _SoftEdge, max(dAz, dEl))

t = 0 inside the window, rising smoothly through the soft edge, t = 1 in the far periphery. Every mode consumes this single scalar. A mode is, in implementation terms, nothing more than a per-fragment colour-and-alpha policy.

Why one object spans all three tiers

The alpha decides which tier you are in

alpha = 0 is Tier 2's absence — native passthrough passes through untouched.
alpha = 1, black, is Tier 2's blackout.
alpha = 1 with a camera-fed colour is Tier 3's re-render.

That is the whole reason the architecture is economical: one sphere and one fragment program reach every capability in the design space. The economy is itself a result.

World-locked, not head-locked

The window belongs to the room

Because azimuth and elevation come from world direction, the window stays fixed to the desk, the doorway, the whiteboard — as the head rotates. A head-locked window would chase the user's attention rather than anchor it, and head-coupled visual-field manipulation is precisely the comfort hazard Norouzi et al. documented.

An optional second lock

True world anchoring for Hard Dark

The base behaviour fixes the window in bearing from wherever the eyes currently are. An opt-in mode raycasts the four painted corners against real geometry (depth API) and stores the world points, re-deriving az/el each frame — so the window stays on a physical object as the user walks toward or around it, growing and shrinking angularly exactly as a real hole in a wall would. Fails gracefully to bearing-lock if any corner misses.

The bug that taught the most · a mono feed for two eyes

Correct in each eye. Broken as a binocular system.

The failure

Frank double vision, on device

The first shader sampled the camera texture by screen position: each fragment looked up the camera pixel at its own device coordinate. Under stereo rendering the same world point projects to different screen positions in each eye — so each eye received a different camera pixel for the same point. The image did not fuse.

The fix

Project world direction through a pinhole model

uv = ((dir·rt, dir·up) / max(dir·fwd, ε))
     / tanHalfFov · 0.5 + 0.5

World direction is eye-independent, so both eyes sample the same pixel for the same point. The mono image behaves like a texture painted onto the world shell, and the visual system fuses it. Half-FOV tangents come from the camera's reported intrinsics (SensorResolution / (2·FocalLength)) rather than an assumed FOV — which is what removed an early slight "zoom" mismatch between periphery and window.

The lesson, stated generally: in stereo rendering, eye-independence of the sampling domain is a correctness criterion, not an optimisation. And where both cameras are live the shader picks between them with a hard split, never an alpha crossfade — crossfading two horizontally offset viewpoints produces exactly the ghosting the scheme exists to avoid.
Chapter 4 §4.4 and failure-museum entry 1. Residual accepted artefact: the painted shell has no true stereo disparity of its own — consistent with the standing constraint that a mono feed must never be presented as geometry-correct stereo across the full field.

The failure museum · documented dead ends are reusable knowledge

Eight things that broke, and what each one taught.

1 · Screen-space camera sampling → double vision

Correct per eye, broken binocularly. → World-direction sampling. Lesson: eye-independence is correctness, not optimisation.

2 · Surface-projected passthrough → deprecated API

The obvious platform mechanism for "put real passthrough on my geometry" was removed at SDK v83. → The window-as-absence composite, which uses only stable compositor behaviour. Lesson: on young platforms, prefer primitives the OS must keep over conveniences it may withdraw.

3 · Shader.Find on device → null

Worked in the editor; silently returned null on Quest, because Android builds strip shaders no serialized asset references. Surfaced as a crashed startup coroutine, not an error. → A serialized template material that runtime objects clone. Lesson: never build device materials from name lookups.

4 · Downsampled detection baking → missing traffic lights

The first ground-truth bake ran the detector on a 640×360 downsample; distant traffic lights fell below detectable pixel size and were silently omitted — an oracle that was not an oracle. → Full-resolution tiled baking, raising coverage to 97% of samples. Lesson: validate ground-truth pipelines against the smallest objects they must contain.

5 · Building into a cloud-synced folder → corrupted builds

Intermittent file-lock failures as the sync client locked freshly written build files mid-process. Lesson: cloud-sync directories are hostile to build systems.

6 · Camera-service screenshots → black frames

The platform screenshot service and the application contend for the same camera resource, so captures came back black whenever the app held the camera. → Framebuffer capture. Lesson, with study consequences: session recordings must use the framebuffer path.

7 · Controller input silently dead at session start

The headset sometimes booted with controllers asleep or the OS stuck in hand-tracking — indistinguishable, from inside the app, from a broken build. → Boot directly into the experiment scene, disable hand tracking, and put "wake controllers" on the pre-session checklist. Lesson: input liveness is an environmental precondition; put it in the protocol, not the debugger.

8 · The magenta floor patch

A metre-wide error-magenta rectangle on the floor: an orphaned line-renderer with a null material, left behind when the video scene was cloned from the passthrough scene. Lesson: when a system evolves by scene-cloning, orphaned objects are a distinct defect class.

Group 1 · modes the literature killed

I built the two most intuitive filters. The evidence says don't ship them.

Retired · was the project's original flagship

Blur — soften the surroundings

The most sophisticated mode in the codebase, and the first one built. Two-ring Gaussian (centre + 8 taps at r + 8 at 2r), whose fine/coarse difference doubles as a local Laplacian driving a world-anchored perceptual noise term (after Tariq 2022) to restore "detectable but not resolvable" high-frequency content, plus a contrast-restoration term (after Patney 2016) because peripheral blur is detected primarily through its contrast loss — restoring contrast roughly doubles the tolerable radius and removes the tunnel-vision percept. Colour mutes before structure softens (desat at t = 0.3, blur at t = 0.5).

Retired anyway. Grogorick's five-way comparison found whole-periphery blur the least subtle technique tested and irritating to 59 of 102 participants; Patney and Walton explain the mechanism (blur announces itself through contrast collapse). I implemented both published mitigations and still could not justify putting it in front of participants as a confirmatory mode.

Never became a mode of its own

Desaturation — grey out the surroundings

The other intuitive design, and the one the ADHD co-design study's participants asked for by name. The eye-tracked 360° comparison is decisive: area darkening guided attention; desaturation alone had almost no effect. Desaturation therefore appears in this system only as one ingredient inside ColorPop's compound re-grading — never as a standalone mode.

And a third thing killed

Head-coupled strength — the obvious way to build it

Making the vignette stronger as the head turns is the natural implementation, and Norouzi et al. found it increases sickness relative to no vignette at all. So this system does the exact opposite — see slide 22, which is also where the ANC analogy stops being a metaphor and becomes a mechanism.

Why this slide exists. Three of the four most obvious ways to build a visual filter are contraindicated by published evidence. That is the strongest possible answer to "isn't this just dimming the screen?" — the design space is full of plausible-looking traps, and the value of the review was learning which ones to walk around.

The salient version, in detail · four levels on two tracks

ColorPop's rule: signal colours stay visible everywhere — merely privileged inside the window.

outside-other < outside-ROG < inside-other < inside-ROG

Colour classification

A shader helper computes membership in the kept red/orange/green set — the colour classes of signal lamps and signage. Hue bands gated by saturation floors, with the warm band given a deliberately higher floor (0.35): warm-white light — headlights sitting at similar hue but low saturation — must not ride into the "orange" class, while genuine lamps and signs sit above 0.5.

The two tracks

Other (non-signal pixels): near-natural inside the window, a slight desaturation keeping it from feeling artificially vivid against its own periphery; graded down to dim grey outside.
ROG: inside, saturation boosted ×1.7 about the pixel's own luminance, Sutton et al.'s published sigmoidal midtone transfer (α = 10, β = 0.5) applied and scaled by (1−t), brightness lifted ×1.15. Outside, ROG keeps its natural colour, merely dimmed — a deliberate decision, because in a monitoring context a peripheral traffic light must never become invisible.

Glare compression, and the guard that saves it

Pixels that are bright, unsaturated and not signal-coloured — the signature of headlight glare — are compressed toward a visible dim spot, never to black. Mild inside the window, strong in the periphery. A blown-core guard protects the one case this would destroy: the clipped white centre of a bright lamp. Before dimming, the shader samples a small Gaussian surround; if the surround is a kept colour — the lamp's saturated fringe — the pixel is exempted.

One more luminance win

The whole periphery is dimmed anyway

Finally everything outside — signal colours included — is multiplied down by 0.85, so the focus window also wins on plain luminance: the single strongest guidance channel in the attention literature. Net outside-signal brightness at defaults is ≈ 0.8 × 0.85 ≈ 0.68 of natural.

A perceptual detail

Why ColorPop's window edge is wider

A colour-to-grey boundary reads perceptually harsher than a darkness boundary, so ColorPop widens its soft edge to 32° against the 20° global default. The transition has to be gentler because the thing being transitioned is more visible.

Group 3 · the temporal layer · where the ANC analogy becomes a mechanism

The filter gets out of the way when you move, and comes back when you return.

The inversion

Norouzi's negative result, run backwards

Coupling vignette strength to head rotation increases sickness. So this system decouples in the opposite direction: the effect fades out while the head moves fast and returns when it settles. This is exactly what ANC does when you lift a cup off the table — it yields to the thing you turned toward.

Thresholds scale with intrusiveness

Blur 30°/s (hold 0.6 s) · ColorPop 35°/s (0.7 s) · Soft Dark 50°/s (1.0 s) · Hard Dark 70°/s (1.5 s). The blackout mode requires the fastest motion to yield and waits longest to return, because wrongly suppressing a strong effect — a sudden flash of the full periphery — is jarring, while wrongly suppressing a gentle one costs nothing.

The asymmetry is a policy, not a tuning artefact

Out in 0.20 s, in over 0.70 s: yielding must feel instantaneous to be trusted; returning may be leisurely because nothing is at stake. And looking away is treated as a possible need for situational awareness (yield generously), while looking back at the task is treated as re-engagement — entering the window plus a 15° margin zeroes the hold timer immediately.

Suppression multiplies; it never resets

A glance over the shoulder during the 3-second formation does not restart the transition — the formation clock keeps counting behind the suppression and the effect resumes at the level it reached. State the user created is never destroyed by a comfort mechanism, only veiled.

The pivot · from building to testing, and a correction to how I first described it

This system does not increase your focus. It reduces what distraction costs you.

The wrong claim

"A filter that makes you more attentive"

Nothing in the mechanism supports this. Dimming the periphery adds no capacity, sharpens no perception, and trains no skill. If you were performing at ceiling in a quiet room, this system has nothing to offer you — and slide 27 shows exactly that happening in the data.

The right claim

"A filter that removes competing demands at the perceptual source"

Diminishment is an extraneous-load intervention. Irrelevant visual information consumes the same limited working memory the task needs; suppress its salience and you return that capacity. The benefit is therefore conditional on there being distraction to remove, and its size should scale with how much there is.

This is not a retrospective softening. It is the pre-registered wording: "the distractor cost is smaller with the vignette on than off." Two questions follow from it, and they are the two tests.

Question 1 · what it buys

Can you perform less badly on a demanding task when there are distractors around you but you have the filter?

Note the framing: not "better than your best", but "less damaged than you would otherwise be". Measured on real hardware, with physical distractors in the participant's actual room.

Question 2 · what it costs

Does a salient version work without costing you spatial awareness?

The dangerous version of a focus aid is one that works by making you blind to everything else. So the second test measures the benefit and the cost in the same task, at the same time — and treats "no meaningful loss of awareness" as a claim that has to survive a test, not a hope.

H1 as registered, Chapter 6 §6.2. The load account is Cognitive Load Theory's extraneous-load construct; the specific prediction that suppressing distractor salience reduces its performance cost is that theory operationalised.

Test design · why these two configurations out of eleven

One static test on real hardware. One dynamic test in simulation. Same people.

Test 1 · Hard Dark, static, live passthrough

The strongest manipulation, in a real room

Why Hard Dark: it is the maximum of the intensity ladder. If the distraction-suppression mechanism exists, this is the condition most likely to reveal it; if it fails here, the gentler modes are not worth testing. It is also the mode with no camera pipeline, so nothing about the result depends on Tier-3 fidelity.

Why real hardware: this is the half neither nearest neighbour could do. Physical distractors, in the participant's own room, seen through actual passthrough.

Test 2 · ColorPop, dynamic, video sphere

The salient version, on identical stimuli

Why ColorPop: it is the only mode that suppresses selectively, so it is the only one where "does it cost awareness?" has an interesting answer. A blackout's answer is trivially yes.

Why simulation: live passthrough can never give two participants the same peripheral events. The video sphere gives identical, repeatable, event-dense stimuli for every participant plus machine-timed ground truth for every probe — onset, position, hit, miss, false alarm.

The pairing is the methodological contribution. Same shader, same window logic, same modes, same interaction — only the source of the periphery differs. So a real-hardware block and a simulation block sit inside the same participants. If they agree in direction, the field learns that the simulation methodology Cheng and McLaughlin were forced into predicts real-hardware outcomes. If they diverge, the divergence is a finding about what simulation misses.
Scope, stated before anyone asks

No claims about driving

The dynamic stimulus is passenger-perspective driving footage, chosen for its event rate and its colour-coded semantic anchors. A seated participant watching video licenses conclusions about attention mechanics under dynamic stimulation — nothing about drivers, driving, or road safety. That scope rule is fixed in the project's framing, not added here.

Chapter 6 §6.1–6.4. The video harness (VideoTestScene) preserves shader, window logic, modes, interaction and detection islands, so a mode behaves identically over video and over the live camera feed.

Test 1 · design · the focus task, and every number in it justified

A 1-back task, distractors in the margins, filter the only thing that changes.

1-back task
Shapes appear one at a time; you press when the current shape matches the one before it. Simple to explain, but it forces you to hold and update an item in working memory on every trial — so it competes for exactly the resource distraction steals.
Commission error
Pressing when you should not have. The measure the distractor literature found sensitive — it moved 1.33 → 3.15 under peripheral distraction while reaction time did not move at all.
Lure
A near-match designed to tempt a wrong press. Added in the later sessions to stop the task being too easy — see the ceiling problem on slide 27.
Within-subjects
Every participant does both conditions, so each person is their own control. With small samples this is where the statistical power comes from — not from headcount.

Test 2 · design · the awareness task, and why the angles are the angles

Probes at known eccentricities — placed where the literature puts them.

Detection-response task (DRT)
A standardised way to measure spare attentional capacity: a probe appears at a scheduled moment and you press as soon as you see it. Slower or missed presses mean less capacity left over. Standardised as ISO 17488:2016, with academic roots in Jahn et al. (2005), who used an 11–23° band.

What we used as probes

Virtual ring markers on authored targets at known azimuth and elevation, so onset time, position, hit, miss and false alarm are all machine-timed ground truth rather than coded from video. 82 targets across the pool; 40 per arm plus practice.

The ruler is the point

Every boundary in our analysis bands is a published anchor, not a choice we made after seeing data. And the shipped window — 6°×4° clear core, 24° falloff — lands on that ruler by construction: clear core stops at the road-centre radius, the gradient spans the probe band, full strength at the edge of the useful field.

Sources · ISO 17488:2016 (DRT). Jahn, Oehme, Krems & Gelau (2005). Victor, Harbluk & Engström (2005) on percent road centre. Ball & Owsley on the useful field of view and its constriction under load. Dose computed from the shipped shader: smoothstep(0, 24, max(|az|−6, |el|−4, 0)).

The confound check · done before the analysis, not after

The markers are pushed through the same filter as the scene.

Verified in the shader and the scene file

The probe respects the filter

The scene ships with the ring-behind-filter flag on: a probe's colour goes through the same desaturation and dimming as everything else at its eccentricity. In the deep periphery it fades the way a real object would.

So in the filter-on condition the peripheral markers were harder to see, not easier.

Why that matters so much

The obvious confound runs the wrong way

The first thing a sceptic should ask about "dimming improved peripheral detection" is whether the probe simply gained contrast against a darkened background — a compositing artefact rather than an attention effect. Because the probe is attenuated with the scene, that explanation predicts the opposite of what we found. Any detection gain has to be paid for out of attention.

This was the third of my supervisor's points from the July review, and fixing it properly turned out to be the difference between a result and an artefact. It is also why the probe design is deliberately engineered against pop-out: ramped onset rather than abrupt (abrupt onsets capture attention automatically), static rather than moving, gradual change (which is famously not captured), and an achromatic pattern that modulates the underlying pixels while preserving its own internal contrast under multiplicative dimming.

Results · Test 2a · the driving task, overall

Overall, the salient filter did nothing. That is the honest headline.

+3.86 pp
overall detection change, all eccentricities pooled

Not significant null

p = 0.44 · improved in only 5 of 11 participants — indistinguishable from a coin flip.

Null hypothesis
The default assumption that the thing you are testing has no effect. A statistical test asks whether your data is surprising under that assumption. Here it is not surprising at all, so the null stands: pooled across all angles, we cannot say ColorPop changed peripheral detection.
Why report it at all
Because the by-angle finding on the next slide would be indefensible without it. Presenting a subgroup result while hiding a null overall result is how false findings get published. The overall null is the context that makes the conditional finding interpretable — and it also means the deck's own claim is narrow.
Two readings, and I cannot fully separate them.

(a) Nothing happened. ColorPop's selective suppression is too gentle to move peripheral detection, and the pooled null is the whole story.

(b) Opposite effects cancelled. The filter's strength varies enormously across the visual field — from 1% of full dose inside 10° to 100% beyond 30°. Pooling every eccentricity into one number averages regions that received almost no manipulation together with regions that received all of it. If the effect is conditional on where the target was, pooling is the one analysis guaranteed to hide it.
Which is why the next analysis exists

The bands were not chosen after seeing this

The four eccentricity bands come from published anchors — the percent-road-centre radius, the peripheral-detection probe band, the edge of the useful field — and they were fixed in the analysis plan by the same ruler shown on slide 26. The dose profile per band is a property of the shipped shader, computable without any data at all.

That does not make the by-angle result confirmatory. It makes it a pre-specified secondary analysis rather than a fishing expedition — and slide 32 gives it the replication test that distinguishes the two.

n = 11 after the single driving-task exclusion. Detection scored against machine-timed ground truth: a hit requires a press within the target's lifetime plus grace, inside a 10° angular radius of its position at the moment of the press.

Results · Test 2b · the same data, split by where the target was

The effect is a conjunction: it needs strong filtering and a target still inside the useful field.

The shape of the result

It is not monotonic in dose — and that is the interesting part

If the mechanism were simply "more dimming helps", the biggest gain would be beyond 30° where dose is 1.00. Instead that band is the only one that goes slightly negative, and the peak sits at 20–30° where dose is high (0.84) but the target is still inside the useful field of view.

Two conditions, both necessary. That conjunction is what distinguishes guidance from simple dimming — and it is a prediction the dose profile makes before the data, not a story fitted to it.

Reading the two quiet bands

Inside 10° and beyond 30°

Under 10° the filter is essentially absent (dose 0.01) — so +4.2 points there has no mechanism to explain it and should be read as noise. Beyond 30° the manipulation is total, the target is outside the useful field, and detection does not improve.

Percentage point (pp)
The arithmetic difference between two percentages. Going from 38.7% to 57.3% is a rise of 18.6 percentage points — not 18.6%, which would mean something much smaller.

A method note that mattered more than expected

The targets move, so "which band was it in?" needed rebuilding

Targets travel a median of 17.2° across their lifetime; 45 of 82 cross a band boundary, and 32% of catches land in a different band than the target's mid-point position. Scoring by mid-point would have blurred the very distinction the analysis depends on. So every target's angular track was reconstructed from the shipped detection bake and each press scored against the target's position at press time. Validated by reproducing the authored coordinates exactly for 75 of 82 targets, and independently against a second logged geometric field.

What this is not. A pre-specified secondary analysis with an overall null above it and n = 11. Statistical power 64%; a study needing n ≈ 16 for the conventional 80%. The replication across phases is the strongest defence available at this sample size — and it is a defence, not a substitute for a confirmatory study.

What it means · one puzzle, three contributions, and the work that follows

Dimming a region made things in that region easier to notice. That needs explaining.

Candidate mechanism — not a result

Attention-gated peripheral sensitivity, plus de-masking

Two published findings make the puzzle coherent. First, peripheral sensitivity in head-mounted displays is attention-gated rather than fixed — what you can detect out there depends on what attention is doing, so freeing attention can raise peripheral detection even as physical contrast falls. Second, visual clutter masks local-contrast probes: flattening the clutter can unmask a signal even while dimming it.

Together: the filter takes contrast away from the probe but takes more away from the things that were hiding and competing with it.

And it is falsifiable

The prediction that would test it

If de-masking is doing the work, the benefit must scale with how cluttered the scene is at the marker's location. Sparse sky, no gain; busy streetscape, large gain. That is computable from the ground-truth detection track already sitting on disk — no new data collection required. It is the first thing I would run next.

A three-tier design space for DR under compositing constraints

Transferable: any future platform is locatable in it by asking which of the three walls it removes.

C2

A working multi-mode DR system on consumer passthrough hardware

Eleven literature-derived modes in one shader, with the window-as-absence hybrid and eye-independent world-direction fusion. The thing both nearest neighbours had to simulate.

C3

First human-factors data on real-passthrough peripheral diminishment

A focus benefit that replicates across two phases; an overall null on peripheral detection; and a conditional band effect with a testable mechanism.

Limits, stated plainly. n = 11 against a planned 20, with 59% and 64% power. The registered vignette × distractor interaction was not tested — distractors were present in every arm. No eye tracker, so head pose is a gaze proxy. The by-angle result is a secondary analysis under an overall null. The awareness cost participants reported is not measured by either task. And no claims are made about driving.

The one-sentence version: a subtractive interface bought a real focus benefit at a cost this study could not measure — and in the ring where its dimming actually lands, it made the periphery easier to see rather than harder.
