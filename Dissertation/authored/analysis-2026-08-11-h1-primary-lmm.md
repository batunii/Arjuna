## Why this file exists

mixed model, `error ~ vignette + (1 | participant)`, with the vignette term as the test and
"odds ratio from the LMM" as the effect size. The paired *t*-test and Wilcoxon are registered as the
**robustness check**.

Chapter 5 as drafted reported only the robustness check. This file supplies the primary test.

## Which estimator, and the honest caveat

Table A.4 says "logistic LMM … + (1 | participant)". A strict frequentist GLMM with *p*-values is
not available in statsmodels, which is why `Tools/analysis/h1_lmm.py` already documents the
substitution: *"logistic GEE … the pragmatic route to 'mixed-model-style' trial-level inference
chosen over statsmodels BinomialBayesMixedGLM (no p-values/CIs in a frequentist frame)"*.

- **GEE** is population-averaged, so its odds ratio is marginal rather than subject-specific, but it
  yields a *p*-value.
- **BinomialBayesMixedGLM** is the literal registered specification but yields a credible interval,
  not a *p*-value.

They agree to the third decimal (0.583 against 0.578), and both agree with the crude odds ratio
(0.576), so the conclusion is driven by the data rather than by the modelling choice. **The
manuscript must state which estimator was fitted** rather than implying a frequentist GLMM with a
*p*-value was run.

## Relation to the registered robustness check

| Test | Role as registered | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Trial-level logistic, vignette term | **PRIMARY** | OR 0.583 [0.396, 0.860], p = .0065 |
| Paired *t*-test on per-participant error rate | robustness | +3.09 pp [+0.62, +5.55], t(16) = 2.66, p = .0172 |
| Wilcoxon signed-rank | robustness | p = .0172-equivalent rank test, dz = 0.645 |

All three agree in direction and significance. **The primary test is more strongly significant than
the robustness check** (p = .0065 against .0172), which is the expected consequence of using
trial-level rather than participant-averaged information.
