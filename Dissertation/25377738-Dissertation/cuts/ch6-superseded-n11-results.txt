\section{Interim Results (N = 11)}

Recruitment continues toward the N = 20 target specified in Section 6.1.3. Twelve people have been
tested so far, across an initial pilot day and three further collection days. Each of the two
confirmatory blocks has eleven usable pairs. These are overlapping but not identical eleven-person
subsets, because one participant contributed a usable result to one block but not the other. What
follows is the interim analysis of those eleven pairs per block. It is reported honestly as interim
for three reasons: it is not the pre-registered confirmatory analysis of Section 6.9, the achieved
power at this N is well below 80\%, and recruitment is still ongoing. One participant's Block B data
is excluded under the pre-registered false-alarm rule (Section 6.9.4). Their filtered run recorded
539 presses that hit nothing, against 40 targets. An additional check confirmed this was
indiscriminate pressing rather than lucky detection: this person's scored catches landed further from
their target on average (median 1.82$^{\circ}$, worst decile 8.62$^{\circ}$) than everyone else's (median
1.59$^{\circ}$), against the 10$^{\circ}$ scoring radius.

\subsection{H1: Block A, workstation focus}

Accuracy on the memory task rose from 92.6\% with the vignette off to 96.3\% with Hard Dark on. That
is a gain of \textbf{+3.65 percentage points} (90\% CI +0.28 to +7.02, paired $t(10) = 2.42$,
$p = .036$, Wilcoxon $W = 8.0$, $p = .024$, $d_z = 0.73$). Nine of eleven participants improved. The
four people tested on pilot day 1 show a smaller gain than the rest: 97.2\% to 99.4\%, +2.20 points,
4 of 4 improved. The seven tested afterwards show 90.0\% to 94.5\%, +4.48 points, 5 of 7 improved.
This split fits a ceiling effect on day 1, not a weaker true effect: at 97.2\% only 2.8 points of
headroom remained to show any gain at all, so the task was made harder for the remaining sessions.
Achieved power at this N and effect size is 59\%. Around 17 participants would be needed for the
conventional 80\%. \textbf{H1 is supported at the interim N}, though the CI's lower bound sits close
to zero, so the effect's true size is not yet pinned down.

\subsection{H2b: Block B, pooled safety check}

Hit rate pooled across the whole field of view rose by \textbf{+3.9 percentage points} with SignPop
on. That figure is not independently reliable on its own (TOST 90\% CI $-4.9$ to $+12.6$ against the
pre-registered $\pm 10$-point margin). The worst case the data support is a loss of about five points,
comfortably inside the safety margin. \textbf{H2b is supported on the safety side}: peripheral
awareness was not lost, and if anything the pooled estimate is positive. The pooled figure only
answers the safety question, because it mixes the near-centre region the filter barely touches with
the far periphery it acts on fully. That is why H2a asks the finer-grained question below.

\subsection{H2a: Block B, the 20–30\textdegree{} band}

Hit rate broken out by eccentricity band shows a pattern, not a uniform shift. Under 10$^{\circ}$ the
change is $-1.1$ points, because the colour-pop gate is close to zero strength there and has nothing
to lift. From 10–20$^{\circ}$ the change is $+2.6$ points, where the gate sits at roughly 20\% strength.
\textbf{From 20–30$^{\circ}$ the change is +18.6 points} (40.0\% to 58.2\%, 90\% CI +2.5 to +34.7,
paired $t(10) = 2.57$, $p = .028$, Wilcoxon $W = 9.0$, $p = .030$, 9 of 11 improved, $d_z = 0.78$,
achieved power 64\%). Beyond 30$^{\circ}$ the change is $-1.5$ points: the gate is at full strength
there, but the target sits far enough into the periphery that a stronger signal buys nothing. Only the
20–30$^{\circ}$ band is individually reliable, and it is the only band whose direction agrees across
both independently collected phases: the pilot phase shows +25.4 points (4 of 4 improved), and the
seven tested afterwards show +14.7 points (5 of 7 improved). The other three bands flip sign between
phases, which is the signature of noise rather than a real effect. This is exactly the shape the
shader's own detection-gated design predicts. The gate has nothing to reveal below 10$^{\circ}$, and it
reveals the most beyond 30$^{\circ}$, but only in the 20–30$^{\circ}$ band is there both a strong effect
to reveal and an eye still able to use it. \textbf{H2a is supported at the interim N, specifically and
only in the band the mechanism predicts.} This is a secondary, exploratory analysis, not a substitute
for a confirmatory test at the final N.

Two further descriptive observations come from the 20–30$^{\circ}$ band. Briefly-visible markers, on
screen under two seconds, went from 2 of 36 caught (6\%) to 9 of 30 (30\%). And the share of catches
occurring while a marker sat in the 10–30$^{\circ}$ region rose from 41\% to 49\%, while the median angle
at the moment of the press barely moved (17.6$^{\circ}$ to 19.1$^{\circ}$). So participants were catching
more of what was already in front of them, not scanning further out to find it.

\subsection{What this interim result does and does not show}

The manipulation-is-a-bundle limitation (Section 6.10.2) applies in full. Nothing above attributes the
20–30$^{\circ}$ gain to the colour lift, the detection aperture, the saturation boost, or the darkened
surround individually. Ten end-of-session questionnaires (Section 6.7) show a divergence worth
flagging rather than resolving here. Perceived focus benefit is strongly positive, 47 of 50 responses
favouring the effects, and the three items on holding attention, looking away, and knowing where to
look were answered the same way by all ten participants. Perceived awareness cost splits close to
evenly, 29 of 50, so about half the responses report feeling cut off from the surroundings or
noticing side events late. Visual comfort sits at 30 of 40 across its four answered items. The
subjective impression of lost awareness is not supported by the behavioural data above, and it is
discussed in Chapter 7.

Achieved power throughout this section is 59\% for H1 and 64\% for
the 20–30$^{\circ}$ band. This means a null result at this N would not have been informative either
way. The results reported here happened to clear their thresholds regardless, which is not the same
claim as having been powered to find them.
